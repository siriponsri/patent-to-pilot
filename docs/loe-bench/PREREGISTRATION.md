## Plain-language summary

Market Window Dossier supports pre-development launch-window planning and gives evaluators a dated evidence pack and verification checklist for freedom-to-operate (FTO) review. The dossier is decision support, not an FTO or legal opinion. Its research benchmark, LOE-Bench, measures capture of future US first final generic approvals under a fixed 20% review budget. Commercial entry, reviewer time saved, Thai demand and freedom to operate each need separate evidence. At each cutoff, the evaluator can review one in five eligible cases, rounded down. A case is a US small-molecule drug history with a documented new molecular entity approval and no first final generic approval as of that date. The outcome is the FDA's first final generic approval within 12 or 24 months. Commercial launch is a separate event.

We compare three forecasts on the same cases. B1 uses time since the original approval. M1 adds information such as listed patents, exclusivity and recorded patent challenges. M3 adds a graph that summarizes those records as OPEN, BLOCKED or CONTESTED. OPEN describes the graph's evidence rule and carries no freedom-to-operate clearance. We count how many cases that later receive a first final generic approval were placed in each review queue. The main comparison is M3 versus B1 at 24 months. A positive result lets us test M3 versus M1 at 24 months, followed by M3 versus B1 at 12 months if that second step succeeds. These comparisons measure queue capture. Reviewer benefit and causal effects are outside the study scope.

The historical check uses only Orange Book editions demonstrably available at each scheduled cutoff. After OSF registration, the main planner runs a gate that checks every required edition and whether at least 50 distinct drugs had an eligible 24-month event. A failed gate leaves all historical hypotheses untested, and the report describes what prevented evaluation. Because prior development work used public data from overlapping periods, the protocol discloses which outputs were generated, which were inspected and how any were used.

The prospective check freezes predictions from the 27 November 2026 source snapshot before later outcome sources are fetched. The team will hash and tag the predictions and add them to OSF before 31 January 2027 at 23:59 UTC. The 12- and 24-month windows end on 27 November 2027 and 27 November 2028. This is a separate test family. It supplies confirmation if the historical analysis did not serve that role and replication otherwise. B3 supplies an expiry-only secondary comparator. Eligible L1 and L1e forecasts and a completed C9 GRPO and supervised fine-tuning pair are exploratory. The biologics track is descriptive.

A ten-percentage-point gain is the reporting threshold for practical usefulness. In simulations with 75 event drugs, a gain of that size was detected about half the time. Development results support studying review prioritization. Cases outside the queue stay in the evaluator's normal process, since none of the development arms established safe auto-clear. ## Study Information

### Research questions and hypotheses

Does the graph-augmented model (M3) capture more upcoming first final ANDA approvals than the age-hazard baseline (B1) at 24 months? Does the graph add capture over the matched flat model (M1) at 24 months? Does M3 also improve capture over B1 at 12 months?

The criteria v3 endpoint is pooled event-row recall at a 20% per-cutoff review budget, using each arm's native score. Test these directional alternatives in this fixed order, with null differences at or below zero.

| Step | Horizon | Alternative |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| H1 | 24 months | M3 recall − B1 recall > 0 |
| H2 | 24 months | M3 recall − M1 recall > 0 |
| H3 | 12 months | M3 recall − B1 recall > 0 |

Use one-sided α = 0.025 at every reached step. The historical pending-alpha entry in the frozen configuration records the decision state before T4c. The predeclared T4c rule resolved that entry to 0.025 for this registration. The frozen file retains its original bytes. Report whether each point estimate reaches +10 percentage points (pp) as a practical-usefulness threshold alongside the superiority decision. ### Unit of analysis, missing data, outliers and weights

The unit is one active-moiety set at a cutoff. A history needs a usable ingredient key, a dated first original final NDA approval on or before the cutoff, and at least one such NDA record by the cutoff whose `submission_class_code` exactly matches `TYPE 1`. That code marks a new molecular entity (NME) for this cohort. A dated first final ANDA on or before the cutoff makes the history ineligible. ## Analyses

### Statistical models

Fit each arm separately by horizon with the fixed TRAIN cutoff and identical eligibility. B1 is the native age-hazard table. Clip age to 0–30 years, use one-year bins, smooth toward TRAIN prevalence with prior strength 5 and use TRAIN prevalence for unseen bins. M1 uses age and age squared, NME, orphan and priority flags, oral and injectable indicators, prior NDA count and the clipped B1 logit. Add years remaining for NCE and other exclusivity, patent count, time to last listed patent expiry, time since earliest patent submission, challenge present/missing, distinct strengths and distinct form/route pairs, and the frozen missingness indicators. Use the ordered feature interface in the configuration. M3 adds one-hot OPEN, BLOCKED and CONTESTED indicators to the same M1 inputs.

The rule asks whether all recorded exclusivities expire by the forecast deadline and whether either all recorded patents expire by then or a qualifying challenge was observed. Each question can have a yes, no, or unknown answer. The combined answer is a model input for forecasting FDA approval. An empty set of dates leaves that part unknown. If the known evidence rules out the condition, the graph returns BLOCKED. If missing evidence prevents a decision, it returns CONTESTED. The exact rule is `exclusivity AND (patent OR challenge)` in Kleene three-valued logic. M1 and M3 both use standardized L2 logistic regression (L2 = 100, 300 epochs, learning rate 0.3, zero initial coefficients and intercept, standardization floor 1e−9). Their native scores are uncalibrated fitted probabilities, with M3 scored before graph actions. B1's native score is its uncalibrated bin probability. [Frozen configuration](https://github.com/siriponsri/patent-to-pilot/blob/66dfa7994fb3dc47772fd36904b8ca5c038fefcd/loe-bench/data/FROZEN_CONFIG.json) · [Graph implementation](https://github.com/siriponsri/patent-to-pilot/blob/66dfa7994fb3dc47772fd36904b8ca5c038fefcd/loe-bench/loe_bench/evidence_graph.py)

For secondary probability summaries and policy, fit one sigmoid per arm/horizon to native logits from whole-drug out-of-fold TRAIN predictions at the latest TRAIN cutoff. Assign five folds by the first eight SHA-256 bytes of the UTF-8 drug key interpreted big-endian modulo five. Clip probabilities at epsilon 1e−8 before logit. Use L2 = 1 and the same iteration count, learning rate, initialization and scale floor as the logistic models. The final native fits use all eligible TRAIN rows and the sigmoid and policy are fixed from inner TRAIN. Exact fitted calibration and diagnostic policy settings are recorded in the configuration. Calibration can change rankings, so the primary queue uses native scores. [Frozen configuration](https://github.com/siriponsri/patent-to-pilot/blob/66dfa7994fb3dc47772fd36904b8ca5c038fefcd/loe-bench/data/FROZEN_CONFIG.json) · [FREEZE](https://github.com/siriponsri/patent-to-pilot/blob/66dfa7994fb3dc47772fd36904b8ca5c038fefcd/docs/loe-bench/receipts/FREEZE.md)

At each cutoff, rank every eligible case by its model's unrounded native risk score and put the highest-scoring fifth in the review queue, rounding the slot count down. If a tie straddles the last slot, give every tied case an equal chance to fill one of the remaining slots. For each year, count how many drug groups with an upcoming first final approval enter the review queue. If four groups tie for two remaining places, each receives half credit. Add the credits across years and divide by the total number of drug-year observations with an upcoming approval. One approval can qualify at two annual cutoffs. That fraction is queue recall. Queue precision divides the same expected hits by the number of slots. To calculate distinct event-drug capture, use independent tie lotteries across cutoffs, calculate each event drug's chance of appearing in the queue at least once on a positive row, and average those chances across event drugs. ### Inference criteria and analysis failures

Use 2,000 paired whole-drug bootstrap draws per horizon with the pinned Python and NumPy versions. Freeze the ascending unique string drug-key order produced by `np.unique(np.asarray(keys, dtype=str))` for each roster and horizon. Use NumPy `default_rng` (PCG64) with seed 20261127 reset per horizon. Each draw samples `n` integer indices with `rng.integers(n, size=n)` for `n` unique keys, with one set of multiplicities shared across arms. Each copy contributes every row of its history and counts separately in distinct event-drug capture. Recompute per-cutoff queue sizes and fractional boundary ties using the unrounded native scores, with fitted models held fixed.

For each reached step, let `differences` be the paired recall differences over all 2,000 draws. The one-sided lower bound is `np.quantile(differences, alpha, method="linear")` at α = 0.025. A step succeeds only if its lower bound strictly exceeds zero. Test H2 only after H1 succeeds, and H3 only after H2 succeeds. A failed or unevaluable step stops the sequence. Earlier successful steps retain their status. Report observed contrasts and two-sided 95% linear percentile intervals where defined, with unreached steps reported descriptively. [Field & Welsh, clustered bootstrap](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00593.x) · [FDA fixed-sequence guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/multiple-endpoints-clinical-trials) · [Queue implementation](https://github.com/siriponsri/patent-to-pilot/blob/66dfa7994fb3dc47772fd36904b8ca5c038fefcd/loe-bench/loe_bench/t1v3.py) · [CP5 decisions](https://github.com/siriponsri/patent-to-pilot/blob/66dfa7994fb3dc47772fd36904b8ca5c038fefcd/docs/loe-bench/PLAN.md#main-planner-decisions-on-cp5-preregistration-critique-27-november-2026)

Any undefined replicate makes the corresponding interval unevaluable. Retain all draws and report the count and cause of invalid draws. An empty event denominator, nonfinite score, failed fit or failed queue check can make a primary step unevaluable. A failed release check, including a required digest mismatch, stops scoring. An undefined secondary metric has no effect on the primary family. Preserve the fixed endpoint and models after test release.
