# CP4 | Which cases reach the evaluator?

**Recommendation recorded before CP4 scoring, 27 November 2026.** Use event recall at a fixed 20% review capacity per cutoff as the primary endpoint. The evaluator can open only a limited number of files each year. Count how many impending first generic approvals appear in that queue. Use precision to tell the evaluator how often a reviewed file contains an event. Approval within the horizon is the benchmark outcome. Actual market launch, legal clearance and reviewer performance require separate evidence.

**Proposed criteria v3, pending owner decision.** H1 tests M3 against B1 at 24 months, H2 tests M3 against M1 at 24 months and H3 tests M3 against B1 at 12 months, all on the same recall endpoint. Advance only after superiority at the preceding step. Propose +0.10 absolute recall as the useful effect for T4, equivalent to ten additional captured event rows per hundred at the same workload. Record statistical superiority and practical usefulness separately. The capacity and useful effect are workflow assumptions for owner review. Development comparisons illustrate their consequences. Choosing a design after inspecting these or earlier validation results constitutes development selection.

## What the previous endpoint rewarded

At the 90% target, M3 answered 991 of 1,083 rows at 24 months. All 991 predictions said no entry. That missed 50 of 50 event rows on which it answered, yet selective error was only 50/991 (5.05%). At 12 months the corresponding counts were 48/48 and 48/1,924 (2.49%). A ranker can genuinely improve the ordering of low-risk cases while doing poorly at finding entries. For p below 0.5, max(p,1−p) orders cases by decreasing confidence in no entry. Averaging accepted-prefix error to 80% mainly rewards putting true negatives early. An always-clear rule with tied confidence has expected error equal to prevalence at every positive coverage under fair tie handling. A low AURC80 alone establishes neither an effective alert queue nor an acceptable missed-entry rate. [K20T1, primary validation operating points](receipts/K20T1.md#t1-operating-points-on-primary-validation)

Traub et al. distinguish the error rate among accepted predictions from the joint probability of an accepted error. AUGRC integrates the latter over coverages. This addresses how to aggregate silent failures. With uniform error costs it still gives no explicit credit for retrieving an impending entry within the evaluator's capacity. We retain the working-point question here. Saito and Rehmsmeier show how precision exposes false-alert burden and how the precision-recall baseline changes with prevalence. Their benchmarks concern classification, not generic approvals. The measured competitors below are the B1 age-hazard table and M1 matched regulatory model. Both papers opened 27 November 2026. ## Three candidates and the claims each can support

Each row is one eligible drug at one cutoff, with a fully observed horizon. Overlapping windows can credit the same first approval at successive annual decisions. Report distinct event-drug capture separately. For each arm use the same as-of information restrictions, row roster, horizon, training dates, cutoff capacity and label-blind tie rule. B1 uses age alone. M1 and M3 use the same regulatory inputs, with verdict indicators added for M3. For every endpoint, compare paired within-horizon arm differences using at least 2,000 common whole-drug resamples. Copy all years of each sampled drug with multiplicity, recompute the statistic, and keep the trained models fixed. Differences describe performance conditional on these fits and observed cutoffs. They omit model-refit, future-year and human-review uncertainty. [DEVX training and features](receipts/DEVX.md) · [Historical graph evidence and missingness](receipts/E7b.md)

1. **Entry recall at 20% review capacity, higher is better.** At cutoff t rank by entry risk, send the first floor(N_t/5) to review, and divide the sum of queued events across cutoffs by all event rows. Queue precision divides the same numerator by occupied slots. At a boundary tie use expected capture under uniform sampling of the required slots from the tied block. A constant score captures roughly 20% of events. Abstention does not remove a row. The queue is ranked before issuing a flag or withholding judgment, and every queued item costs one slot. M3's graph can affect risk through its verdict features. H2 consequently measures incremental queue ranking, while the effect of the action override requires a policy endpoint. Resample and rerank within each cutoff. Few event drugs, floor rounding, large B1 ties and discordant cases near the boundary can make differences discrete. T4 must reproduce tie masses and paired capture discordance. Annual ranking assumes simultaneous access to that year's eligible roster. Waiting time and varying review effort are separate operational measurements.
2. **Average precision (AP), higher is better.** Pool eligible rows at one horizon, rank by entry probability and sum precision at each distinct threshold times the corresponding increase in recall. This stepwise AP groups ties and gives every observed event equal weight. Every model supplies a risk before abstention. AP measures the verdict contribution to ranking, with no direct effect from subsequent hard actions. Recompute the whole curve on each paired draw. AP can distinguish rankings that agree at 20% and can be unstable when a few early hits or cutoff-to-cutoff score shifts dominate. Report horizon prevalence alongside AP and annual AP as a descriptive sensitivity where estimable. T4 would need the joint score tails and ties across years and models. AP by itself assigns no particular importance to staffed capacity.
3. **Expected decision loss per eligible row, lower is better.** Give a missed auto-clear cost 10, a false flag cost 1, any referral cost 0.5 and a correct automated action cost 0. Sum costs and divide by all eligible rows. These illustrative relative costs include a perfectly resolving referral. For calibrated probabilities, expected losses for clear, flag and refer are 10p, 1−p and 0.5. Thus clear at p ≤ 0.05, flag at p ≥ 0.50, refer between. Apply these same costs, thresholds and calibration opportunity to B1, M1 and M3. In this illustration M3 inherits the current BLOCKED override (clear below hi and refer high-risk conflicts). Show workloads and a future M3-without-override ablation to isolate its contribution. Paired cluster resampling estimates the cost difference. A few missed clears can dominate its variance. T4 would need calibrated risks, verdicts, class-conditional action frequencies and the joint distribution of losses. The loss depends on unmeasured relative costs, review quality and available staff. A policy exceeding the capacity is infeasible even if its loss is low.

At 24 months the 2013–2014 cutoffs contributed 1,083 rows from 561 drugs, 91 events (8.40%) from 67 drugs, and 215 slots. The 24-month row prevalence exceeds the approximate planning range. ## T1 replacement and operational acceptance

Keep the embargoed K07/E7b training pipeline. Fit the raw models on TRAIN and calibration maps on pooled whole-drug out-of-fold TRAIN predictions. Use the same folds and opportunity for B1. Retain the failed T1 configuration as historical evidence. Freeze the top-fifth **ranking rule** separately from automatic actions. It has exactly the stated capacity by design. A row outside the queue has no implied safety label.

For a three-action pilot, define FLAG as an actionable alert delivered directly, REFER as a request for evaluator review and CLEAR as low entry risk. If flags require evaluator review at deployment, count them against the same budget and measure total staffed demand. Before fitting thresholds the owner must identify which actions consume a slot, the maximum acceptable event rate among clears, and minimum acceptable flag precision. At each horizon enumerate independent observed lo < hi pairs on TRAIN out-of-fold probabilities. Use probability ≤ lo to clear, ≥ hi to flag, and intermediate risk to refer. For the proposed M3 safety variant route BLOCKED/high-risk conflicts to review and apply the same lo limit to automatic clears, including BLOCKED cases. Keep UNKNOWN explicit. Log the current override separately as a control.

Select among pairs meeting the safety, flag precision and workload constraints on TRAIN. Target the 20% referral budget, prefer the feasible rate closest to it from below, then greatest event capture by flags, then smaller lo and larger hi. Require nonempty clear and flag sets and report denominators. Freeze the pair and evaluate once on development validation. Use a one-sided upper cluster bound on event rate among clears, a lower bound on precision among flags, and report the fraction of all events wrongly cleared. Zero observed failures or few contributing drugs require an independently validated rare-event bound or an inconclusive safety result. A percentile bootstrap of zero observed failures gives false certainty. Report realized referral and staffed load by cutoff with counts of flags, clears, conflicts, overflow and referrals by outcome. A cutoff over budget or a failed safety/precision gate fails operational acceptance. Hold overflow pending review. Do not silently auto-clear it. Retain the fixed-capacity ranking comparison even if an automatic policy fails. A successful ranking alone authorizes no automatic clearance.
