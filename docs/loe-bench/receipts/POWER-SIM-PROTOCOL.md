## Synthetic histories and scores

Each jurisdiction begins with 651 eligible drug histories. Draw integer starting ages from a geometric distribution on zero and up with mean 17 years, cap at 30, and retain ages above 30 during follow-up while scoring them in the age-30 bin. The initial age law is an assumption. New age-zero histories arrive uniformly within annual blocks, at a Poisson rate of `22 × 651 / 539` per year. All first events are absorbing within jurisdiction. Draw one drug frailty as a unit-mean lognormal with log SD 0.5, shared across its origins and jurisdictions. Use the 24-month DEV B1 score levels as age-specific relative hazards after conversion by `−log(1−p)/2`. Hold the hazard constant within each year at the age at the year's midpoint. Scale it to the assumed initial 24-month event count using the age distribution and Gaussian quadrature over frailty. In shock scenarios, multiply each annual hazard by a unit-mean lognormal shared by every drug in that jurisdiction and year. Simulate time to first approval from an exponential cumulative-hazard clock. No other exits are modelled. Reuse the same event time in all windows `(origin, maturity]`, so adjacent 24-month quarterly windows share 21 months. Include eligible arrivals and keep remaining histories at successive origins. Measure realized roster retention, event rows, distinct drug-jurisdiction approvals, unique event drugs, and event multiplicity.

Use T4c age-clock B1 ranks with exact ties. Use the T4c Gaussian rank regression on age bins, B1 rank and synthetic event status, averaging M1 and M3 coefficients and class-specific scales. Set the centered development year term to zero. Combine a persistent drug effect and independent origin-specific residuals with variance fractions 0.8 and 0.2 in the central simulation. Reuse residuals across horizons at the same origin. Use the fitted cross-arm correlations 0.96697 (24/36 months) and 0.97301 (12 months). A score drift stress changes the cross-arm residual correlation to 0.90. For the 36-month horizon reuse the 24-month rank fit as a transport assumption. Shift synthetic event scores for M1 and M3 by arm-specific constants calibrated on an independent stream using the exact cutoff-specific score breakpoints from T4c. This imposes joint score/outcome distributions for planning. The resulting scores are not forecasts or fitted models.

For each design and nuisance setting, target H1 effects of +10 and +20.37 pp and H2 effects of 0, +1.36, +2 and +5 pp on a full crossing. M3 recall targets B1 recall plus H1, and M1 recall targets M3 recall minus H2. Hold H3 at +10 pp in A. Verify simulated population contrasts on independent panels to absolute tolerance 0.005. Check empirical differences for both nulls without using any evaluation draw to adjust the shifts. Also stress a +10 pp H2 gain in B and C, low and high 24-month incidence, within-drug score correlation 0.5 and 0.95, a jurisdiction/year shock log SD of 0.30, lower cross-arm correlation and a faster/younger roster (starting age mean 10 and twice the arrivals).
