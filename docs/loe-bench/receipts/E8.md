# E8 | NBER annual Orange Book editions

## Result

**PASS for ingestion and descriptive audit.** The adapter adds 31 annual editions. The mixed inventory has 38 of 39 January cutoffs within six months of their ordering dates. Twenty-three additional development years pass the year-end-proxy screen, including 2011. A PDF title page can establish how far the data extend or identify the edition. The proxy supplies neither exact historical publication evidence nor measured data currency.

## Protocol

Select the freshest available edition by edition date, using archival FDA ZIPs for exact ties. Require a verified capture or exact publication evidence for ZIPs. Use the annual edition's documented publication date where known and December 31 of its printed edition year as the ordering proxy otherwise. Admit a year-only edition on January 1 of the following year. Keep the existing six-calendar-month freshness floor and disclose where the proxy drives it.

Audit one January 1 cutoff per year in 1987–2025. Use FIX2c for identity linkage. Prior to 2005 the annual tables report a single ingredient per drug, so require an exact NDA and product-number anchor for canonical composition. Count a distinct NDA as linked when any product links and also report linked product coverage.

For 2009–2016, compare each annual edition to the latest available inventory ZIP with a member date in the same calendar year. Also compare to the closest inventory ZIP by edition date to the annual snapshot's expected December 31 prior-year reference, if within 120 days. Collapse product numbers for both comparisons and report exact set intersection over union and both directional rates for NDA–patent–expiry and NDA–exclusivity code–expiry. Count shared identities whose expiry sets differ. Report unclassifiable types separately. Keep the existing development validation starts and strict embargo when counting additional eligible training years. No modeling.

After the first literal-identifier comparison, inspection of source records identified the FDA ZIP's `*PED` marker for pediatric extensions. The normalized agreement strips only that suffix and retains the expiration for each listing. The audit also preserves unnormalized patent agreement for comparison.

## Failures and limitations

- The web lookup returned HTTP 403 for the NBER landing page and could not resolve the DOI directly. The landing page loaded through its public HTTP endpoint, and the journal record and manuscript resolved through NLM. Exact annual publication days remained unverified in the guide and checked title pages. FDA's general January schedule is a separate indication of publication timing. PDF creation timestamps establish file creation. The annual titles checked are listed with hashes in the source manifest. [FDA guidance, Q3 and footnote 20](https://www.fda.gov/media/160167/download) · [Journal record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38130474/)
- The known content ages are much larger than the one-day ordering proxies. The 1987 title page is current through 31 January 1987, yielding 335 days at the January 1988 cutoff. The 2010 annual title page is current through **31 December 2009**, yielding **366 days** at the January 2011 cutoff. The other checked year-end title dates likewise give ages of 366–367 days at the selected cutoffs. A later ordering proxy can rank above a contemporaneous ZIP. The December 2010 monthly ZIP remains missing. [E2b source search](E2b.md#evidence-and-failed-retrievals) · [FDA 2010 title page](https://data.nber.org/fda/orange-book/historical/1986-2016/1_orange_book_PDFs/full_books_1980-2016/2010.pdf)
- NBER coverage is conditional on having a listed patent or exclusivity. The source omits drugs without recorded protection and can miss listings that appear and disappear between annual snapshots. Earlier single-ingredient rows use exact product anchors and the historical approval date of these identity mappings remains unverified. The crosswalk has 2026 source vintage. Patent term extensions, pediatric extensions, short-lived listings and cleaning discrepancies can alter dates and coverage. The adapter uses each row's original edition field and its recorded expiry. [NBER README §§3–4](https://data.nber.org/fda/orange-book/historical/1986-2016/README.pdf) · [User guide §§3–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10731339/)
