We are building LOE-Bench to forecast the first final US FDA approval of an abbreviated new drug application (ANDA) within 12 or 24 months of an annual cutoff. We compare an age-based clock, a calibrated flat logistic model, and a matched model with a three-valued regulatory evidence graph that can refer uncertain cases. Our primary measure is area under the risk–coverage curve over the first 80% coverage. A separate descriptive track covers biosimilars.

Find original empirical studies and openly inspectable datasets that address three questions.

1. Which studies predict time to first generic or biosimilar submission, patent challenge, regulatory approval or commercial launch? Distinguish these endpoints and time origins. Include survival models, deterministic patent clocks and economic predictors that could genuinely be known at forecast time. Trace the CPT study (10.1002/cpt.1479) and the 2018 Value in Health and 2019 JLME studies by Beall and colleagues to their primary records.
2. Which selective prediction, failure-ranking, calibration or learning-to-defer methods improve decision triage relative to confidence ranking? Identify evidence for small, imbalanced tabular datasets, time shift and clustered observations. Distinguish a better ranking across coverage levels from a safer operating point at one threshold.
3. Which open data releases or licensed repositories can reconstruct historical Orange Book or Purple Book snapshots, challenge signals, reference-product presentations and demand? For each, locate a dated public release, archival coverage, join fields, revision and reuse terms. Explain how an analyst could establish what was public on a specific cutoff.

Return a study table with citation, DOI or stable primary link, endpoint and time origin, population and exclusions, data and features, method and comparator, metric and reported result, and separate columns for as-of reconstruction and forward time-split validation (yes/no/unclear with evidence). Report censoring and unit of splitting. Include a second table for datasets with edition, release-date proof, licence, row locator and leakage limitations. Give one exact page, table, figure or methods locator per substantive claim. Label inaccessible methods or missing dates UNVERIFIED. Distinguish articles from abstracts and avoid counting the same cohort twice.

Exclude the FDA First Generic list as a predictor. Exclude hindsight features, including later approvals, backfilled challenge dates, subsequent litigation outcomes, and sales windows indexed to a future event. An approval date, claims period or filing date alone does not establish when a record became public. Identify closed-source inputs explicitly. End with three low-cost ideas suitable for a matched development-only comparison and one reason each could fail.
